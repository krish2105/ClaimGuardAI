# Prior Authorization Requirements

## Clause CG-PA-001: Procedures Requiring Prior Authorization

The following CPT categories require prior authorization before the service
is rendered, across all plan tiers: advanced imaging (MRI, CT, PET, CPT
71260/72148/74176), cardiac procedures above diagnostic ECG (CPT
93306/93458/92928/93017), maternity delivery (CPT 59400/59510) and major
gynecological surgery (CPT 58150), endoscopic procedures (CPT 43235/45378),
oncology treatment (CPT 96413/77301), orthopedic surgery (CPT 29881/25605),
sleep studies (CPT 95810), epidural injections (CPT 62323), cataract surgery
(CPT 66984), dental crowns (CPT D2740), and blood transfusion (CPT 36430).
Claims for these codes submitted without a valid prior-authorization
reference should be treated as incomplete, not automatically denied for
fraud, unless other fraud indicators are also present.

## Clause CG-PA-002: Emergency Exemption

Prior authorization is waived when the procedure is performed as part of an
emergency department encounter (CPT 99284/99285) and the claim documentation
indicates the treatment was medically urgent. The Coding Agent and Policy RAG
Agent should treat a missing prior-authorization reference as non-punitive in
these cases, provided the ICD-10 diagnosis is consistent with an emergency
presentation.

## Clause CG-PA-003: Authorization Turnaround SLA

Standard prior-authorization requests must be adjudicated within 5 business
days for Basic and Enhanced plans, 3 business days for Comprehensive, and 24
hours for Thiqa members. Failure to respond within the SLA window results in
automatic deemed-approval of the request for Basic, Enhanced, and
Comprehensive members only; Thiqa requests are never deemed-approved and
must receive an explicit adjudication.

## Clause CG-PA-004: Retrospective Authorization

Retrospective (after-the-fact) authorization requests are accepted only for
emergency admissions and must be submitted within 10 calendar days of the
treatment date. Retrospective requests submitted after this window are
denied under this clause and the claim should be marked as requiring human
escalation rather than an automated denial, since the underlying medical
necessity has not been assessed.
